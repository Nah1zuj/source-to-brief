# Design Intelligence Brief: Trial-to-Paid Conversion Storyline

Reading path: synthetic competitor scan + synthetic market memo -> leadership narrative -> deck

## 1. Decision Context

Support a product strategy deck deciding whether to reposition the onboarding experience around time-to-first-value instead of feature breadth.

## 2. Source Inventory

| Source | Role | Reliability Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Synthetic competitor scan, 8 SaaS onboarding pages | Category pattern evidence | Synthetic example; feature observations only |
| Synthetic market memo from sales team | Demand and objection signals | Synthetic example; stakeholder evidence, not verified analytics |

## 3. Problem Frame

The supplied packet suggests that buyers understand the category but struggle to see how quickly the product can create value for their own workflow.

## 4. Evidence-Backed Insights

| Insight | Evidence | Confidence | Design / Product Implication | Source Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Competitors sell speed and setup confidence more than feature depth. | 6 of 8 synthetic competitor entries lead with setup time or guided workflow. | Medium | Lead deck narrative with activation speed. | No conversion data supplied. |
| Sales objections cluster around migration effort. | Synthetic sales memo lists migration as a recurring objection. | Medium | Include migration proof points and onboarding support. | No call transcripts supplied. |
| Feature comparison alone is weak evidence for urgency. | Competitor scan shows similar feature sets across category. | Low | Use feature parity as backdrop, not headline. | No buyer research supplied. |

## 5. Opportunity Areas

- Reframe onboarding around "first usable workflow in one session."
- Add proof points for migration support.
- Test a deck storyline that moves from category parity to activation confidence.

## 6. Artifact Translation

Deck:

- Narrative thesis: buyers need confidence that adoption will be fast, not another feature grid.
- Slide claims: category parity is high; setup confidence differentiates; migration concerns block purchase; activation proof can reduce perceived risk.
- Strongest evidence: competitor scan pattern plus recurring synthetic sales objections.
- Recommended storyline: market sameness -> buyer anxiety -> onboarding confidence -> proof plan -> decision ask.

## 7. Assumptions, Risks, And Unknowns

- Assumption: the synthetic sales memo reflects common buyer concerns.
- Risk: competitor messaging may not reflect actual conversion drivers.
- Unknown: trial-to-paid conversion by onboarding path was not supplied.

## 8. Reusable Blocks

- Problem statement candidate: Buyers understand the category but lack confidence that setup will be fast enough to justify switching.
- Artifact-ready claim: Activation confidence may be a stronger wedge than feature breadth.
- Deck question: What proof would make a buyer believe value arrives in the first session?
